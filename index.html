01 · The Screening Bottleneck

Cheap Static Math vs. Real Circuit-Level Noise

Physical qubits fail constantly from gate errors, stray heat, and noisy measurements. A quantum error-correcting (QEC) code protects a smaller number of reliable logical qubits by spreading them across many noisy physical data qubits, watched over by helper ancilla qubits that repeatedly check parity without reading the quantum state itself.

Modern search loops — including AI- and RL-guided discovery — can propose thousands of candidate quantum LDPC codes in hours. Properly testing even one candidate at the circuit level (simulating tens of thousands of noisy runs in Stim and decoding every syndrome with BP+OSD) takes minutes to hours. Measuring just 242 small codes in M0 took 76.3 CPU core-hours.

To cope, search loops screen candidates first with a back-of-the-envelope formula: Φ = k · d² / n. The catch is that Φ is blind to hardware reality — it rewards packing more logical qubits k while ignoring how heavy, deep, and noisy the error-checking circuit becomes.

02 · Structural & Circuit Physics

Tanner Graphs, Short Cycles & Syndrome-Extraction Depth

Every CSS quantum LDPC code is defined by two sparse binary parity-check matrices over GF(2): H_X (detects Z phase flips) and H_Z (detects X bit flips). Drawn as a network, each matrix forms a Tanner graph connecting data qubits to parity checks.

Computing the 28 features in m0_features.parquet takes milliseconds per code, yet captures the physical mechanisms that Φ misses:

  • Check weight (check_weight_mean): Directly sets how many sequential two-qubit CX gates each check executes per round — controlling both gate-error count and idle qubit decoherence time.
  • Short loops (cycle4_count, cycle6_count, girth): Four-step cycles in the Tanner graph bounce Belief Propagation messages back to themselves, causing iterative decoders to stall and fall back to slow OSD elimination.
  • Graph expansion & schedule cost (lambda2, cx_total, circuit_ticks): Captures global connectivity alongside exact gate counts under the pinned X-then-Z monomial-matching schedule.

03 · Ground-Truth Measurement & Cost Asymmetry

Why the Best Codes Cost 40× More to Verify

Every M0 label was measured under one exact, hashed protocol (protocol_hash 6230a7a8…): uniform depolarizing noise at p = 0.002, Z-basis memory for r = d_upper rounds, sampled in Stim 1.16.0 and decoded with ldpc 2.4.1 BP+OSD (min-sum, 30 iterations, scaling 0.625, OSD-CS order 10).

To keep statistical error bars tight without wasting CPU hours on weak codes, each code runs in 256-shot batches until it records at least 100 logical failures, stopping at a cap of 40,960 shots. If a code hits 40,960 shots with fewer than 100 failures, INV-3 refuses to report a thin point estimate: the row is marked censored (true_ler = null), excluded from regression training, and ranked conservatively by its 95% Wilson upper bound (true_ler_ub).

This creates a stark cost asymmetry: across all 242 codes, the median code finished in just 1,024 shots — whereas the 10 best codes required 24,320 to 40,960 shots each. Good screening prevents burning those 40,000-shot budgets on bad candidates.

05 · Diagnosis of the Headline Fail

One Held-Out Fold Decided All Ten Winners

Why did LightGBM score an impressive 0.871 Spearman across all 242 codes, yet catch only 2 of the top 10? The answer is structural — not measurement noise (the label-noise ceiling is a perfect 1.000).

Because random row splits leak near-duplicate siblings from the same polynomial recipe (INV-2), M0 evaluates strictly leave-one-program-out across the 11 construction programs. As it turned out, all 10 of the lowest-error codes came from one single template, bb_v1_pair_2_2 (weight 4, k = 2). When pair_2_2 is held out, LightGBM trains exclusively on heavier templates (w ≥ 5) and must extrapolate cold to every single top-10 winner in one fold.

Meanwhile, Φ scored exactly 0.000 because every top-10 code has k = 2 and Φ ≤ 1.64 — buried behind at least 70 higher-k, higher-Φ codes with worse error rates.

06 · Scientific Rigor & What Comes Next

Six Automated Invariants & the Path to M1

Scientific ML benchmarks rarely fail loudly; they fail silently through data leakage, mixed simulation settings, or rounded-up claims. Every core rule in CONTRACT.md is enforced automatically by pytest (427 default tests passing):

INV-1 · Truth ≠ Surrogate

Separated Measurements

true_* labels and pred_* model outputs never share a file or column. A prediction is never presented as a real error rate.

INV-2 · Leakage Guard

Grouped Splits Only

Random train_test_split on code rows is blocked by source-grep tests. Splits group by construction_program_id (and by family from M2).

INV-3 · Censoring

Bounds, Not Thin Guesses

Fewer than 100 failures triggers censored = True and true_ler = null; excluded from fits and ranked by true_ler_ub.

INV-4 · Normalization

One LER Formula

1 − (1 − P_L)^(1/(r·k)) lives exclusively inside protocol.py, guarded by a self-tested source-grep check.

INV-5 · Distance Honesty

Bounds Never Called Exact

Randomized search produces d_upper (d ≤ d_upper). d_exact stays null unless provably exact; proxy is phi_from_d_upper.

INV-6 · Protocol Hash

No Mixed Protocols

Every row carries a SHA-256 protocol_hash. Metric and training functions raise ValueError if two protocols are ever combined.
